DL_POLY export (FIELD, CONFIG, CONTROL)

The typed system as DL_POLY 4 input in units of kcal/mol, in DL_POLY's own conventions: harmonic bonds and angles with DL_POLY's ½k (twice the force field's k), class II bonds and angles as quartic terms (2K2, 3K3, 4K4), torsions as cos terms (OPLS triples as cos3) with the 1-4 electrostatic and van der Waals scale factors on exactly one term per 1-4 pair (a zero-amplitude term where the force field has no torsion), impropers in the rule's own atom order (explicit rules before wildcards; the outer atoms read by index, backwards, then otherwise), class II out-of-plane terms as harmonic inversions with the centre first, and every van der Waals type pair written out (lj, nm 9-6 for class II, buck). Consecutive identical molecules share one molecular type (nummols N); CONFIG holds the atoms about the cell centre; CONTROL is a generic NVT run with the same cut-off.

$$\begin{gathered}\text{harm:}\ \ \tfrac{1}{2}k(r - r_0)^2 \qquad \text{quar:}\ \ \tfrac{1}{2}k(r - r_0)^2 + \tfrac{1}{3}k'(r - r_0)^3 + \tfrac{1}{4}k''(r - r_0)^4 \\ \text{cos:}\ \ A\left[1 + \cos(m\phi - \delta)\right] \qquad \text{cos3:}\ \ \tfrac{1}{2}\left[A_1(1 + \cos\phi) + A_2(1 - \cos 2\phi) + A_3(1 + \cos 3\phi)\right]\end{gathered}$$

Universal force field (UFF)

Every element from H to Lr, typed from its bonds, hybridisation and oxidation state. Used for clean-up and for any structure the other force fields cannot type (metals, silicon, phosphorus, halogens).

$$\begin{gathered}E = \sum k(r - r_0)^2 + \sum K\left[C_0 + C_1\cos\theta + C_2\cos 2\theta\right] + \sum \tfrac{1}{2}V\left[1 - \cos(n\phi_0)\cos(n\phi)\right] \\ \quad + \sum D\left[\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)^{12} - 2\left(\frac{x}{r}\right)^{6}\right] + E_\text{inversion}\end{gathered}$$

Rappé et al. 1992

Charge equilibration (QEq)

Charges that equalise the electronegativity of every atom at fixed total charge, for any element. The energy below is minimised; the linear system is solved by conjugate gradients.

$$\begin{gathered}E(q) = \sum_i \chi_i q_i + \frac{1}{2}\sum_i J_i q_i^2 + \sum_{i<j} q_i q_j K(r_{ij}) \\ K = J_{ij}^{\text{Slater}}(r)\ \text{(default)} \quad \text{or} \quad K = \frac{14.40}{\sqrt{r^2 + a_{ij}^2}}\ \text{eV}, \qquad a_{ij} = 7.20\left(\frac{1}{J_i} + \frac{1}{J_j}\right)\ \text{Å}\end{gathered}$$

Rappé & Goddard 1991; the Slater integral J_ij(r) is given under QEq for periodic fillers. The Ohno–Klopman kernel is an option, its difference from 1/r switched off over a fixed 5 to 7 Å

QEq for periodic fillers

Options of the charge equilibration for crystals and fillers such as silica, ZnO, clays and CaCO3 when the force field gives no charges. Kernel: the Coulomb integral between the atoms' normalised ns Slater densities, as in the original QEq. Here n is the element's period, ζ = λ(2n+1)/(2R) with R the QEq radius and λ = 0.4913, and the integral is evaluated exactly through incomplete gamma functions and a radial quadrature, then tabulated per element pair. Ewald, the default for a cell periodic along a, b and c: the long-range 1/r is summed over every periodic image. By default a large cell, where the reciprocal sum would cost more than 2·10⁷ atom × k-vector terms per step (a few thousand atoms), takes the tapered kernel over the images within the cut-off instead and says so; Ewald asked for explicitly is always used. The real space erfc(αr)/r runs over explicit images out to the cut-off, so the cell may be narrower than twice the cut-off. The reciprocal space is applied as structure factors and never stored. The kernel's short-range difference from 1/r is added for every image pair within the cut-off. Hydrogen: its hardness follows its charge, solved self-consistently with damped steps. Bounds: per element a lowest and highest charge; atoms that would pass one are held there, the rest are re-equalised, and held atoms are released when the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions no longer keep them.

$$\begin{gathered}J_{ij}(R) = \iint \frac{|\phi_i(\mathbf r_1)|^2\,|\phi_j(\mathbf r_2)|^2}{r_{12}}\,d\mathbf r_1\,d\mathbf r_2,\qquad \phi \propto r^{n-1}e^{-\zeta r},\quad \zeta = \frac{\lambda(2n+1)}{2R_i} \\ K^{\mathrm{Ewald}}_{ij} = \sum_{\mathbf n}{}' \frac{\operatorname{erfc}(\alpha|\mathbf r_{ij}+\mathbf n|)}{|\mathbf r_{ij}+\mathbf n|} + \frac{4\pi}{V}\sum_{\mathbf k\neq 0}\frac{e^{-k^2/4\alpha^2}}{k^2}\cos(\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r_{ij}) - \frac{2\alpha}{\sqrt\pi}\,\delta_{ij},\qquad J_H(q) = J_H^0\left(1 + \frac{q}{\zeta_H}\right)\end{gathered}$$

the Slater integral tends to 1/R once the densities part; the Ewald split α = √(−ln ε)/r_c and k_max = 2α√(−ln ε) for the accuracy ε

General AMBER force field (GAFF)

Atom types for organic molecules assigned by rules in antechamber's order (rings, aromaticity, conjugation), with AMBER functional forms: harmonic bonds and angles, cosine torsions, 12–6 Lennard-Jones, 1–4 interactions scaled by ½ (LJ) and 1/1.2 (Coulomb).

$$\begin{gathered}E = \sum k_b(r - r_0)^2 + \sum k_\theta(\theta - \theta_0)^2 + \sum \frac{V_n}{2}\left[1 + \cos(n\phi - \gamma)\right] \\ \quad + \sum 4\epsilon\left[\left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{12} - \left(\frac{\sigma}{r}\right)^{6}\right] + \sum \frac{q_i q_j}{4\pi\epsilon_0 r}\end{gathered}$$

Wang et al. 2004

Gasteiger–Marsili charges (PEOE)

Partial equalisation of orbital electronegativity: charge flows along each bond from the less to the more electronegative atom, in six iterations damped by half each time, so it stays local.

$$\chi_i = a_i + b_i q_i + c_i q_i^2, \qquad \Delta q_i^{(k)} = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\sum_j \frac{\chi_j - \chi_i}{\chi^+}$$

Gasteiger & Marsili 1980

Water models and their massless sites

Three-site models (SPC, SPC/E, SPC/Fw, TIP3P and its variants, OPC3) carry their charges on O and H. Four-site models (TIP4P, TIP4P-Ew, TIP4P/2005, TIP4P/Ice, OPC) move the negative charge to a massless site M on the H–O–H bisector. TIP5P puts it on two massless lone pairs L out of the molecular plane, on the side away from the hydrogens. The sites are placed from O and H at every evaluation and their forces handed back to them by the chain rule; for the out-of-plane lone pairs the cross product adds one more term to the virial.

$$\begin{gathered}M = (1-\alpha)\,O + \tfrac{\alpha}{2}(H_1 + H_2), \qquad \alpha = \frac{d_{OM}}{r_{OH}\cos(\theta/2)} \\ L_\pm = O + a\,(\mathbf{r}_1 + \mathbf{r}_2) \pm c\,(\mathbf{r}_1 \times \mathbf{r}_2), \qquad a = -\frac{d_{OL}\cos(\phi/2)}{2 r_{OH}\cos(\theta/2)}, \qquad c = \frac{d_{OL}\sin(\phi/2)}{r_{OH}^2 \sin\theta}\end{gathered}$$

r₁, r₂ the O–H vectors, θ the H–O–H angle, φ the L–O–L angle; for TIP5P (r_OH 0.9572 Å, θ 104.52°, d_OL 0.70 Å, φ 109.47°) a = −0.344908 and c = 0.64438 Å⁻¹, GROMACS's virtual_sites3 function 4 (c in nm⁻¹ = 6.4438); the cross part scales as λ² when the atoms are scaled by λ, hence the extra virial term

Charges from bond increments

Class II and CVFF force fields (PCFF, COMPASS, CVFF and IFF from their .frc files) give each bonded pair of atom types an increment that moves charge from one atom to the other. Summing over the bonds gives charges that balance bond by bond, so every neutral molecule stays neutral and a charged group keeps its formal charge. CAPS assigns them whenever the force field carries a bond-increment table and the charges are the force field's own.

$$q_i = \sum_{j \text{ bonded to } i}\delta_{ij}, \qquad \delta_{ji} = -\delta_{ij}$$

Force fields by group and crystal potentials

Each group of molecules (a filler and its matrix, each component of a blend) is typed and parameterised by its own force field with its own charges and mixing rule; the Lennard-Jones pairs between groups follow a cross rule chosen for them, or pairs given explicitly, and every cross pair is written out in the engine files. A crystal group can instead take a literature many-body potential (Tersoff, Stillinger–Weber, Vashishta, EAM) that LAMMPS reads from its file. Units of the LAMMPS files: automatic (metal — eV, ps, bar — when a potential is read by LAMMPS in metal units only: AIREBO, AIREBO-M, REBO, MEAM; the published file then unchanged and every force-field energy parameter divided by 23.060549, LAMMPS's own factor; real — kcal/mol, fs, atm — otherwise, where LAMMPS converts Tersoff, SW, Vashishta, GW and EAM files itself), or real with AIREBO / REBO (a copy of the file CAPS converts: A, B and the LJ, torsion and Morse ε; the splines are dimensionless), or metal for any system. Both are exact: the physics does not depend on the unit system, only LAMMPS's electric-conversion constants differ in their last digits. MEAM maps each element to a library entry (by default the first of its atomic number) and keeps the parameter file's index order. A group's force field may change its molecules: TraPPE O2 and N2 add a massless centre site, a united-atom force field folds hydrogens into their carbons. That is done on the real structure before the groups are typed, in every frame and as one undo step, only on that group's molecules; molecule ids are the ones the structure had before, other groups keep their order, and assigning again changes nothing. The report gives each group its massless sites, the sites added, the atoms folded and its net charge. In LAMMPS a massless site has a form in two cases: four-site water (the tip4p styles), and a site on a rigid molecule, every bond and angle constant 0 as in TraPPE's O2 and N2. The second is written as an atom of a body that fix rigid carries, with mass 0.001 g/mol taken from the atoms that define it, so the molecule's mass and centre of mass are exact; zero-force bonds exclude the pairs inside the body, from PPPM too. Bodies are held during minimisation, have their own thermostat (fix rigid/nvt/small) and stay out of the fix that integrates group mobile; a box of nothing but such molecules takes fix rigid/npt/small under pressure. `velocity create` gives a linear body too much kinetic energy at the start, which the thermostat removes within its damping time. A site on a flexible molecule or out of the plane (TIP5P) has no LAMMPS form and is refused by name: export those to GROMACS.

$$\begin{gathered}\epsilon_{ab} = \sqrt{\epsilon_a\epsilon_b}\ \text{ or }\ \frac{\epsilon_a + \epsilon_b}{2}, \qquad \sigma_{ab} = \frac{\sigma_a + \sigma_b}{2}\ \text{ or }\ \sqrt{\sigma_a\sigma_b} \\ \text{sixth power:}\ \ \sigma_{ab} = \left(\frac{\sigma_a^6 + \sigma_b^6}{2}\right)^{1/6}, \qquad \epsilon_{ab} = \frac{2\sqrt{\epsilon_a\epsilon_b}\,\sigma_a^3\sigma_b^3}{\sigma_a^6 + \sigma_b^6}\end{gathered}$$

Cells too small for their bonds

A force field reads each separation at the nearest image and excludes or scales bonded partners there, and a bond list has one entry per pair of atoms. A small crystal cell breaks both: a polyethylene cell repeats its chain every 2.5 Å, so a carbon bonds to two images of one neighbour and partners three bonds apart lie closer through the next cell. CAPS follows every path of two and three bonds and compares where it ends with the image the engines would read.

$$\mathbf{f} = \mathsf{h}^{-1}\sum_{\mathrm{path}} \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{bond}}, \qquad |f_k| < \tfrac{1}{2}\ \text{ for every periodic axis } k$$

The test follows from the minimum-image convention; f is the path's end in fractional coordinates. A tie at ½ fails, because two images are then equally near.

Fitting a force field to crystal data

Pair, bond and charge parameters of the assigned force field are refined so that a crystal (a filler, a mineral, a polymer crystal) keeps its observed structure and reproduces its lattice energy and elastic constants. Each crystal is evaluated in its periodic copies: a supercell at least twice the cut-off wide whose copies move together, so a small cell still sees every neighbour within the cut-off. Shells, whose positions are not observed, are relaxed in every evaluation. In conventional fitting the observed structure should be a stationary point, and its residual forces and stresses are fitted to zero. In relaxed fitting the cell and atoms are relaxed for each trial set (L-BFGS over the atoms and the six symmetric strains, the strain gradient −W F⁻ᵀ from the virial tensor) and the relaxed cell lengths, angles and coordinates are fitted to the observed ones; this weighs a soft direction by how far it moves the structure, not by its small gradient. Elastic constants are central differences of the stress over ± strain with the ions relaxed; bulk and shear moduli are their Hill averages. The weighted sum of squares is minimised by Levenberg–Marquardt with a forward-difference Jacobian, and standard errors come from s²(JᵀJ)⁻¹ when there are more observations than parameters. The fitted values can go into the assignment or into a force-field file (an explicit rule of the same types is updated, otherwise a rule for exactly those types is appended).

$$\begin{gathered}S(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_i w_i\left[y_i^{\mathrm{calc}}(\mathbf{p}) - y_i^{\mathrm{obs}}\right]^2, \qquad \left(J^{\mathsf T}J + \lambda\,\mathrm{diag}\,J^{\mathsf T}J\right)\delta\mathbf{p} = -J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{r} \\ \boldsymbol{\sigma} = -\frac{W}{V}, \qquad \frac{\partial E}{\partial F} = -W F^{-\mathsf T}, \qquad C_{IJ} = \frac{\sigma_I(+\varepsilon_J) - \sigma_I(-\varepsilon_J)}{2\varepsilon}, \qquad s_k = \sqrt{\frac{S}{m-n}\left[(J^{\mathsf T}J)^{-1}\right]_{kk}}\end{gathered}$$

When to use it

Filler potentials (CaCO3, silica, ZnO, clays, a Buckingham or shell-model set from a paper) that must hold the measured lattice and stiffness before they meet a polymer; refitting one pair after a cross-term or charge change; checking what a published set gives for a crystal it was not fitted to (no parameters ticked: the crystal is only evaluated).

Departure from the reference
static (0 K, no zero-point or thermal expansion: fit to low-temperature data); no vibrational frequencies, dielectric or piezoelectric constants as targets; no breathing shells or many-body terms among the fitted parameters; the Ewald grid is refined to 0.5 Å for a fit so nearby cells are compared consistently
