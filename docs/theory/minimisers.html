L-BFGS

A quasi-Newton minimiser that approximates the inverse Hessian from the last few steps (two-loop recursion). It is the fastest of CAPS's minimisers for smooth energies of thousands of atoms.

$$d = -H_k\nabla E, \qquad H_k \text{ from the last } m \text{ pairs } \left(s_i = x_{i+1} - x_i,\; y_i = \nabla E_{i+1} - \nabla E_i\right)$$

Liu & Nocedal 1989

The default minimiser. After growth with a contact scale below 1, run the capped push-off first (Relax does by default).

FIRE

A damped dynamics minimiser: velocities are mixed towards the force direction and the time step grows while the power F·v stays positive; the motion stops when it turns uphill.

$$\begin{gathered}v \leftarrow (1 - \alpha)\,v + \alpha\,|v|\,\frac{F}{|F|} \\ \text{if } F\cdot v < 0:\quad v = 0,\;\; \Delta t \leftarrow f_\mathrm{dec}\,\Delta t,\;\; \alpha = \alpha_\mathrm{start}\end{gathered}$$

Bitzek et al. 2006

f_inc, f_dectime-step growth and cut1.1, 0.5
N_minsteps before growth5

Conjugate gradients and steepest descent

Steepest descent follows the force; Polak–Ribière conjugate gradients mixes in the previous direction so successive steps do not undo each other.

$$d_{k+1} = F_{k+1} + \beta d_k, \qquad \beta = \max\left(0,\; \frac{F_{k+1}\cdot(F_{k+1} - F_k)}{F_k\cdot F_k}\right)$$

Polak & Ribière 1969

Capped-force push-off

Overlapping atoms (from growth with lowered contact limits, or packing) make Lennard-Jones forces explode. The push-off caps them: inside the radius where the force would exceed the cap the potential becomes linear, and the cap is raised stage by stage while minimising. Optionally the push-off runs first as NVT dynamics, as in the paper: the cap is raised geometrically over a set time (the λ ramp) so chains move apart by motion before the minimisation stages.

$$\begin{gathered}F_\mathrm{LJ}(r) \to \operatorname{sign}(F)\,\min\left(|F_\mathrm{LJ}(r)|,\, F_\mathrm{cap}\right), \qquad F_\mathrm{cap} = 5, 20, 100, \dots, F_\mathrm{final}\ \text{kcal/mol/Å} \\ \text{MD:}\ \ F_{\mathrm{cap},k} = 5\left(\frac{F_\mathrm{final}}{5}\right)^{k/9}, \quad k = 0, \dots, 9, \ \text{each for } t_\mathrm{ramp}/10\end{gathered}$$

after Auhl et al. 2003

Cell relaxation with held lattice parameters

The cell and the atoms relax together while chosen lattice parameters keep their values: the chain axis of a polymer crystal, β of a monoclinic cell, or a layer spacing. The variables are the free ones of a, b, c, α, β and γ, and the atoms in fractional coordinates. The cell keeps its orientation: h = R₀ h_std(a, b, c, α, β, γ), with R₀ fixed at the start and h_std putting a along x and b in the xy plane. L-BFGS minimises the enthalpy H = E + PV with its exact gradient. For the atoms that is hᵀ ∂E/∂x. For the cell it is ∂H/∂h = (PV I − W) h⁻ᵀ at fixed fractional coordinates, W being the virial tensor, carried to each lattice parameter through ∂h/∂p. Where the force field has the long-range tail correction, its energy E_t ∝ 1/V enters through its own volume derivative (a virial of E_t on each diagonal component), not the fluid tail pressure, so the gradient is that of the energy minimised. A cell narrower than twice the cut-off is evaluated as copies that move together, so every neighbour within the cut-off is seen.

$$H = E + PV,\qquad \frac{\partial H}{\partial \mathbf s_i} = \mathbf h^{\mathsf T}\frac{\partial E}{\partial \mathbf x_i},\qquad \frac{\partial H}{\partial \mathbf h} = \left(PV\,\mathbf I - \mathbf W\right)\mathbf h^{-\mathsf T},\qquad \frac{\partial H}{\partial p} = \sum_{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial \mathbf h}\right)_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial h_{\alpha\beta}}{\partial p}$$

s fractional coordinates, x = h s; p one of a, b, c, α, β, γ; with the tail correction W carries E_t (not P_t V) on its diagonal

Reaction paths and the nudged elastic band

A path between two structures of the same atoms. B's atoms are matched to A's when the file lists them in another order. Atoms of one element, and of one bonded environment when both structures have the same environments, are assigned by the Hungarian method to the least summed squared distance. With the environments, that assignment is then turned into the nearest map that keeps every bond, so a symmetric molecule's hydrogens never trade carbons. A molecule is first superposed from the four orientations of its principal axes, the assignment and the fit repeated until stable; a periodic cell uses minimum-image distances. A molecule's B is superposed on A, so the path carries no rigid motion. The images come from straight lines, from LST (each image minimises the deviation of its pair distances from distances interpolated between the ends, weighted by 1/d⁴, plus a small pull to the straight-line image), or from IDPP (the same distance objective without the pull, the images optimised together as a band). The climbing-image nudged elastic band then finds the minimum energy path and its saddle with the force field. Each image feels the force perpendicular to the path plus a spring force along the improved tangent. Once the band has settled, the highest image climbs if its energy is above both neighbours (the ends count); otherwise no image climbs and the report says so. The images move by FIRE, and a band that grows past ten times its starting length, or whose energies stop being finite, is stopped with a note.

$$S_\kappa = \sum_{i<j}\frac{(d^{\kappa}_{ij} - d_{ij})^2}{d_{ij}^4},\qquad \mathbf F_i = \mathbf F_i^{\perp} + k\left(|\mathbf R_{i+1} - \mathbf R_i| - |\mathbf R_i - \mathbf R_{i-1}|\right)\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}_i,\qquad \mathbf F_{\mathrm{climb}} = \mathbf F - 2(\mathbf F\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol\tau})\hat{\boldsymbol\tau}$$

LST adds γ Σ |r − r^LI|² with γ = 10⁻⁶ bohr⁻⁴; τ̂ is the tangent to the higher-energy neighbour (energy-weighted at extrema)
